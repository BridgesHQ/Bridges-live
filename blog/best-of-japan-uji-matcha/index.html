The best of Japan, in a bowl: why Uji matcha matters

From Kyoto’s ancient tea gardens to your morning ritual — the story of ceremonial matcha, and why Bridges brings the finest of Japan to the world.

Bridges Matcha
Ceremonial matcha · Uji, Kyoto · JAS Organic
Last updated: September 2026

Bridges is about connecting worlds, and few things connect a moment the way a bowl of true ceremonial matcha does. Bridges Matcha sources first-harvest, stone-ground matcha from Uji in Kyoto — the region widely regarded as the birthplace and enduring standard of Japanese tea, with centuries of practice behind every harvest.

Why Japan — and why Uji

Japan has refined tea into an art over a thousand years. Uji, just south of Kyoto, is where that art reached its peak: the climate, the soil, and generations of master growers produce a depth of flavor and a vivid jade color that define what people mean by quality matcha. When you drink Uji matcha, you’re tasting the source.

What makes ceremonial grade different

Ceremonial-grade matcha comes from the youngest, first-harvest leaves, shade-grown and stone-ground into an ultra-fine powder meant to be whisked with water and drunk on its own — not blended into a latte or dessert. The result is smooth and naturally sweet, without the bitterness of lower grades. It is the grade made for the tea ceremony, and for a genuinely calm, focused moment.

The Bridges idea, in a bowl. One side is centuries of practice. The other side is now. Matcha is the space in between.

The ritual — how to enjoy it

Sift a small scoop, add water just off the boil, and whisk in a brisk zig-zag until frothy. Drink it slowly. That is the whole ritual — and the point. A few quiet minutes, done well, change the whole day.

Bringing the best of Japan to the world

Bridges Matcha connects you directly to Uji growers — retail tins for home, plus wholesale and café supply, fulfilled worldwide through Bridges HQ. It’s part of Bridges World: the same trust and provenance behind everything we do, from real estate to global trade.
